Vyber privacy policy

Effective 22 November 2026

Your applets and everything you save in them stay on your phone. To build or change an applet, Vyber sends what you write to its builder, which uses Anthropic's Claude, and only after you've agreed. There's no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

What stays on your phone

Your applets, the entries, photos and settings you save in them, your comments on applets, and your reminders are kept in Vyber's private storage on your device. Applets have no way to reach the internet, so none of this is sent to me or anyone else. Deleting an applet deletes what it saved, and deleting Vyber deletes everything. If you back up your phone with Apple, the backup may include them, under Apple's own terms.

What goes to Vyber's builder

The first time you ask for a new applet or a change, Vyber shows what it sends and asks you to agree. Nothing is sent before you do, and you can withdraw in Settings → AI builder. When you ask, Vyber sends its builder:

  • what you write: your description of a new applet or a change, or a comment and the earlier messages in that comment;
  • for a change, the applet as Vyber built it (its code, not what you saved in it);
  • for a comment, the part of the applet you pointed at: which lines of the applet's code drew it, and its size and colours. Never a picture of your screen or the words on it.

The builder passes these to Anthropic's Claude, which writes the applet and then checks it against what Vyber is allowed to make. Anthropic handles them under its commercial terms, which don't allow it to train its models on them.

Vyber's builder keeps what you wrote only until the build ends, and the finished applet until your phone picks it up, 24 hours at most. The builder's one-line summary of what it did is deleted 24 hours after the build ends. What stays is a record that a build happened, with its size and what it cost.

Knowing who is asking, without an account

Vyber doesn't ask your name or email. To keep your credits yours and stop people spending the builder's money, it uses Apple's own proofs instead:

  • the App Store's ID for your download of Vyber. It's the same for your Apple Account on every device, and doesn't reveal who you are;
  • a key Vyber creates on your iPhone with Apple's App Attest, which shows that requests come from a genuine copy of Vyber;
  • Apple's DeviceCheck, which lets Vyber mark with Apple that an iPhone has had its free credits.

Vyber's server keeps that ID and key, when you first and last opened Vyber, and whether you've had the free credits. Your network address is used only in the moment, to limit how often new copies can start, and isn't stored.

Credits and purchases

Apple takes the payment for credit packs and Vyber Plus through the App Store. I never see your name, email or payment details.

Your balance is kept by RevenueCat, a purchase service, under the App Store ID above. It receives your purchase receipts and history, and technical details: app version, operating system version, device model, store country and language, and your IP address, as with any internet connection. It uses them to keep your balance and confirm what you've bought.

Reports

If you report an applet from its menu, Vyber sends the reason you chose, your note if you wrote one, the applet as Vyber built it and what was asked for to make it, never what you saved in it. Reports are read by me and deleted after 90 days.

Sending an applet to a friend

When you choose Send to a friend, Vyber sends a copy of the applet as Vyber built it (its code, name and look, never what you saved in it) to Vyber's server, which checks it the way it checks every new applet and makes a link. Your own Messages or Mail opens with the link written in: you choose who gets it, and Vyber never sees their name, number or address. Anyone with the link can see the applet's name and description and add their own copy, which starts empty. The copy on the server is kept while the link works; it's deleted if the link is taken down, and you can email me to have your links taken down. The link carries an anonymous code instead of anything about you, so whoever you send it to can block links from you on their phone.

An applet someone sent you keeps what you save in it apart from your other applets, on your phone. You can report it or block links from whoever sent it.

Camera, photos, sharing and reminders

An applet can open the camera, the photo picker or the share sheet only right after you tap something in it, and Vyber asks for each permission when it's first needed. Photos you add are kept in Vyber's storage on your phone and are never uploaded. Text an applet shares goes only where you choose in the share sheet. Reminders are scheduled on your phone by Vyber; nothing about them is sent anywhere.

What Vyber never touches

Your location, contacts, microphone, health data or advertising ID. Vyber doesn't track you across other apps or websites, and has no ads.

Where it's processed

Vyber's server runs at DigitalOcean in Toronto, Canada. Anthropic and RevenueCat process data in the United States.

Children

Vyber is rated 13+ and isn't directed to children under 13. Its builder declines anything unsuitable for that age.

Your choices

You can withdraw your agreement to the builder in Settings → AI builder; your applets keep working. You can delete any applet, and what it saved, at any time. To have the server's and RevenueCat's records about you deleted, email me; your remaining credits go with them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. If Vyber starts sending something new to its builder, it asks you to agree again first.
